## 1. Executive Summary

Mission NR-056 investigated two critical questions regarding the evaluation infrastructure introduced in NR-055:
1. **The Item Count Discrepancy:** Why is the benchmark file named `benchmark_v2_100.json` while containing exactly 95 evaluation items?
2. **Evaluator Reliability:** Does `robust_evaluator.py` eliminate false positives without introducing false negatives across English, Devanagari Hindi, and Hinglish?

---

## 2. Forensic Resolution of the "100 vs 95" Discrepancy

- **File Inspected:** `data/evaluation/benchmark_v2/benchmark_v2_100.json`
- **Manifest Inspected:** `data/manifests/benchmark_v2_manifest.json`
- **Creation Script Inspected:** `experiments/forensics_nr055/build_benchmark_v2.py`
- **Audit Finding:**
  - In `build_benchmark_v2.py`, the author planned 100 prompts across 8 categories:
    - FQA: 15
    - MTH: 15
    - INS: 15
    - LOG: 10
    - RC: 10
    - HIN: 15
    - HNG: 10
    - CTX: 5
    - Subtotal: $15 + 15 + 15 + 10 + 10 + 15 + 10 + 5 = \mathbf{95\text{ prompts}}$.
  - The script allocated 5 prompts to Context Retention (`CTX-01` to `CTX-05`) instead of 10 prompts, leaving the total at 95 items while naming the output file `benchmark_v2_100.json`.
  - **Verdict:** No items were dropped, corrupted, or deleted during evaluation. Exactly 95 items were generated, and all 5 candidate models in NR-055 were evaluated on the exact same 95 items.

## 3. Evaluator Adversarial Test Suite Results

A 25-case adversarial test suite (`experiments/forensics_nr056/evaluator_adversarial_results.json`) was executed across all evaluation types:

| Metric | Measured Value | Analysis |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Total Test Cases** | 25 | Comprehensive coverage across English, Math, Hindi, and Constraints |
| **Exact Tier Accuracy** | **92.0%** (23 / 25) | High precision across standard test inputs |
| **False Positive Rate** | **0.0%** (0 / 25) | Successfully rejected `'ice'` in `'service'`, `'red'` in `'hundred'`, `'2'` in `'2024'` |
| **False Negative Rate** | **4.0%** (1 / 25) | Identified a critical regex boundary limitation on Devanagari script |

### Critical Evaluator Discovery: Devanagari Word Boundary Defect
- Standard Python ASCII regex word boundaries (`\b`) do not recognize Devanagari unicode characters as word characters (`\w`).
- As a result, `re.search(r'\bनई दिल्ली\b', text)` returns `False` even when `नई दिल्ली` is preceded or followed by Devanagari characters or Danda punctuation (`।`).
- **Impact on NR-055 Scores:** This defect caused Hindi answers generated by models to receive Score 0 under substring matching, introducing a mild false-negative bias against valid Devanagari generation.

---

## 4. Evaluator Improvements for Benchmark V3

For Benchmark V3, the evaluator must be upgraded to:
1. Use whitespace/punctuation boundary matching rather than standard ASCII `\b` for Devanagari text.
2. Incorporate explicit negative assertion filtering to detect negations (e.g. *"not Paris"*).
